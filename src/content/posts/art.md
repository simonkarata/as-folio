The history of art traces how humans have used images, objects, and spaces to express ideas, beliefs, and emotions across time. From prehistoric cave painting to contemporary installation art, artistic traditions reflect the values and concerns of the societies that created them.

Ancient civilizations such as Egypt, Mesopotamia, Greece, and Rome produced works that served religious, political, and ceremonial purposes. In the medieval period, religious art became central to European life, shaping architecture, manuscript illumination, and sacred decoration.

The Renaissance brought renewed interest in classical learning, humanism, and realism, while later movements such as Baroque, Romanticism, and Realism expanded the range of emotional and social expression in art. In the modern era, artists challenged tradition through Impressionism, Cubism, Surrealism, and abstraction, redefining what art could be.

Today, art continues to evolve through new media, technologies, and global influences. Contemporary practice often explores identity, memory, politics, and the changing relationship between viewers and images.
