Music has existed in human societies for thousands of years, serving as a means of ritual, storytelling, celebration, and emotional expression. From early rhythm and chant to complex orchestral and electronic forms, music has evolved alongside human communities and technologies.

In ancient civilizations, music was closely linked to religion, ceremony, and communal life. Instruments such as flutes, drums, lyres, and horns helped shape early musical traditions, while oral transmission preserved melodies and forms across generations.

During the medieval and Renaissance periods, music became more structured, with the rise of sacred compositions, courtly traditions, and the development of notation. The Baroque, Classical, and Romantic eras expanded musical expression through greater complexity, emotional intensity, and new forms such as opera, symphony, and sonata.

The 20th and 21st centuries brought rapid change through recording technology, radio, film, jazz, rock, hip-hop, electronic music, and global digital platforms. These developments made music more accessible, more hybrid, and more interconnected across cultures.

Today, music continues to evolve as a global art form, shaped by innovation, migration, and new media. It remains one of the most powerful ways people express identity, memory, and shared experience.
