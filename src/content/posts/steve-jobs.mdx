Steve Jobs was an American entrepreneur best known for helping build Apple into one of the world's most influential technology companies. His career connected personal computers, animated film, digital music, smartphones, and consumer software. Jobs was a prominent public figure, but the products associated with him were created by large teams of engineers, designers, artists, and business leaders.

## Early Years and Apple

Jobs co-founded Apple Computer with Steve Wozniak and Ronald Wayne in 1976. The Apple II, introduced the following year, helped bring personal computers into homes, schools, and businesses. Its success showed that a computer could be a practical consumer product rather than a machine used only by specialists.

Apple introduced the Macintosh in 1984. Its graphical interface, mouse, and integrated design helped popularize a more approachable way to use a computer. These ideas had a longer history and were developed across the computing industry, but the Macintosh brought them to a wider audience through Apple's hardware and software.

Jobs left Apple in 1985 after disagreements within the company. He later founded NeXT, which developed computers and software for higher education and business. Although NeXT's hardware did not become a mass-market success, its software platform was later acquired by Apple and became the basis for important parts of macOS.

## Pixar and a Return to Apple

Jobs also became a major investor and leader at Pixar. The studio's first feature-length computer-animated film, _Toy Story_, was released in 1995. Pixar's success demonstrated how advances in computer graphics could support expressive storytelling, while reflecting the work of its artists, animators, and technical teams.

Apple acquired NeXT in 1996, and Jobs returned to the company. He became interim chief executive in 1997 and later served as CEO. During this period, Apple simplified its product line and developed a series of products that reshaped its business, including the iMac, iPod, iPhone, and iPad.

The iPhone, launched in 2007, combined a phone, an internet-connected device, and a multi-touch interface. Its influence extended beyond Apple: it accelerated the shift toward smartphones built around touchscreens, mobile software, and app marketplaces. The iPad, introduced in 2010, brought a similar approach to a larger portable screen.

## Product Focus and Leadership

Jobs was known for emphasizing a coherent experience across hardware, software, and services. Apple often controlled many parts of that experience, which allowed close integration but also gave the company significant control over its platforms. This approach helped create polished products, while raising ongoing questions about openness, repair, competition, and the power of platform owners.

His leadership style was demanding and sometimes controversial. Accounts from colleagues describe high expectations and intense debate, alongside an ability to persuade teams to pursue ambitious goals. A balanced view of his career should recognize both his influence on product direction and the contributions of the people and organizations around him.

## Influence and Legacy

Jobs died in 2011, but the companies and products he helped shape continue to affect how people work, communicate, listen to music, and use digital media. His legacy is not simply a story of one person's vision. It also reflects decades of progress in computing, design, manufacturing, software, and entertainment—and the collaboration required to turn those advances into products used around the world.
