# History of Art

## Introduction

Art has been a fundamental part of human culture for thousands of years. It preserves ideas, beliefs, emotions, and visual experiences across generations.

## Ancient Art

Early civilizations such as the Egyptians, Mesopotamians, Greeks, and Romans produced art for religious, political, and ceremonial purposes. Monumental architecture, sculpture, and painting reflected the values and power of their societies.

## Medieval Art

During the Middle Ages, art in Europe was strongly shaped by Christianity. Illuminated manuscripts, mosaics, stained glass, and Gothic cathedrals became important forms of artistic expression.

## Renaissance

The Renaissance marked a renewed interest in classical learning, humanism, and realism. Artists such as Leonardo da Vinci, Michelangelo, and Raphael created works that emphasized anatomy, perspective, and individual achievement.

## Baroque and Rococo

Baroque art was dramatic, emotional, and theatrical, often used to impress viewers. Rococo followed with lighter, more decorative, and intimate styles.

## Romanticism and Realism

In the 19th century, artists explored emotion, nature, and the human experience. Romanticism celebrated imagination and passion, while Realism focused on everyday life and ordinary people.

## Modern Art

Modern art broke away from traditional rules and techniques. Movements such as Impressionism, Cubism, Surrealism, and Abstract Expressionism challenged viewers to see art in new ways.

## Contemporary Art

Contemporary art includes a wide range of styles, media, and ideas. It often addresses identity, politics, technology, and global culture.

## Conclusion

The history of art is a record of how humans have imagined, observed, and expressed the world around them. It continues to evolve as new cultures and technologies shape creative practice.
